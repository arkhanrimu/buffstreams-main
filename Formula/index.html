F1 Season Overview

The Formula 1 season typically runs from March to December and features over 20 Grand Prix races. Each race weekend includes free practice sessions (FP1, FP2, FP3), qualifying, and the main race. Some weekends also feature Sprint races, which are shorter races that determine the starting grid for the main Grand Prix. The 2024 season features 24 races across five continents, making it one of the longest seasons in F1 history.

Key F1 Races

F1 fans can look forward to several iconic races throughout the season:

  • Monaco Grand Prix - The crown jewel of F1, held on the streets of Monte Carlo. Known for its tight corners, tunnel, and harbor views. The most prestigious race on the calendar.
  • British Grand Prix (Silverstone) - The home of British motorsport, known for its high-speed corners like Copse, Maggotts, and Becketts. One of the original F1 circuits.
  • Italian Grand Prix (Monza) - The Temple of Speed, known for its long straights and passionate Tifosi fans. The fastest circuit on the calendar.
  • Belgian Grand Prix (Spa-Francorchamps) - The greatest racing circuit in the world, featuring the iconic Eau Rouge/Raidillon corner combination.
  • Japanese Grand Prix (Suzuka) - The only figure-of-eight circuit on the calendar, known for its challenging corners and passionate fans.
  • Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (Yas Marina) - The season finale under the lights, featuring the famous hotel with a track passing through it.
  • Singapore Grand Prix - The original F1 night race, held on the Marina Bay street circuit.
  • US Grand Prix (Austin) - The race at the Circuit of the Americas, purpose-built for F1 in Texas.

F1 Championship

The F1 World Championship is split into two categories: the Drivers' Championship and the Constructors' Championship. The Drivers' Championship is awarded to the driver who scores the most points throughout the season, while the Constructors' Championship is awarded to the team whose two drivers score the most combined points. Points are awarded to the top 10 finishers in each race: 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1. An additional point is awarded for the fastest lap if the driver finishes in the top 10.
