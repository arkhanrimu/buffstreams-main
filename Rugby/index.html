International Rugby

International Rugby is the pinnacle of the sport, with nations competing for prestige and glory in the world's biggest tournaments. The Rugby World Cup is the most prestigious event in the sport, held every four years and featuring the top 20 nations in the world.

Rugby World Cup

The Rugby World Cup is the premier event in international Rugby, held every four years since 1987. The tournament features 20 nations competing over six weeks, with the final typically held in October or November. New Zealand (All Blacks) has won the tournament three times (1987, 2011, 2015), while South Africa (Springboks) has won it four times (1995, 2007, 2019, 2023), making them the most successful nation in Rugby World Cup history. The 2023 Rugby World Cup in France was won by South Africa, who defeated New Zealand 12-11 in the final.

Six Nations Championship

The Six Nations is the premier annual international Rugby competition, featuring England, France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland, and Wales. The tournament is held every year from February to March, with each team playing five matches. The Grand Slam is awarded to the team that wins all five matches, while the Wooden Spoon is given to the team that finishes last. Ireland won the Grand Slam in 2023 and 2024, while France won it in 2022. The Six Nations is one of the oldest international Rugby competitions, dating back to 1883.

Pacific Nations Cup

The Pacific Nations Cup is an annual international Rugby competition featuring Pacific Island nations and other teams. The tournament features teams including Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Japan, and the United States. It serves as a qualifying tournament for the Rugby World Cup and provides valuable international experience for emerging Rugby nations.

British & Irish Lions Tours

The British & Irish Lions tour every four years, alternating between Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa. The Lions are a combined team from England, Ireland, Scotland, and Wales, and their tours are considered one of the greatest spectacles in Rugby. The 2025 Lions tour to Australia will be the next edition, while the 2021 tour to South Africa was won by the Springboks 2-1.

Club Rugby Competitions

Beyond international Rugby, several domestic and cross-border club competitions attract top talent and massive viewership:

English Premiership

The English Premiership is the top tier of English Rugby, featuring 10 teams competing from September to June. The league is known for its physicality and competitive nature. Key teams include Saracens (6 titles), Exeter Chiefs (2 titles), Leicester Tigers (11 titles), and Harlequins (2 titles). The Premiership final is held at Twickenham Stadium in London.

United Rugby Championship (URC)

The URC is a cross-border competition featuring teams from Ireland, Wales, Scotland, Italy, and South Africa. The league was formed in 2021 from the merger of the Pro14 and other competitions. Key teams include Leinster (4 titles), Munster (3 titles), Stormers, and Bulls. The URC has become one of the most competitive Rugby leagues in the world.

Top 14 (France)

The Top 14 is the top tier of French Rugby, featuring 14 teams competing from September to June. The league is known for its physicality and passionate fan base. Key teams include Toulouse (23 titles), Clermont, Racing 92, and Bordeaux. The Top 14 final is held at the Stade de France in Paris.

Super Rugby

Super Rugby is the premier club competition in the Southern Hemisphere, featuring teams from New Zealand, Australia, and the Pacific Islands. The competition was restructured in 2022 to feature 12 teams. Key teams include Crusaders (11 titles), Blues, Hurricanes, and Chiefs. Super Rugby is known for its fast-paced, attacking style of play.

Champions Cup & Challenge Cup

The European Rugby Champions Cup is the premier club competition in the Northern Hemisphere, featuring the top teams from the Premiership, Top 14, and URC. The competition is organized by European Professional Club Rugby (EPCR) and features 24 teams. Leinster has won the most titles (6), followed by Toulouse (5). The Challenge Cup is the second-tier European competition, featuring teams that did not qualify for the Champions Cup.
